What we do

Today, Living Proof Labs takes early consulting and design partnerships: define the user stories a deployment depends on, agree what evidence would count, and plan verification before a team bets on a site. It is aimed at humanoids first, and at any robot stack after that.

Platform capabilities below are the design we are building. They are not in operation.

User stories as the contract

A capability will be a user story—a clear, testable behavior customers and operators care about. No vague “AI magic.” Concrete expectations.

Simulation worlds that grade

A software build will run in a simulation matched to those stories. Each run earns a grade per story, so a team can see where it passes, where it regresses, and what to fix next.

Capability history, not one-off demos

Grades will accumulate in a library: the same robot across software versions, the same software on different hardware, benchmarks against peers. The result is a grade for the whole system, and a paper trail partners can trust.

We improve teams, not weights. The first product is meant to coach the developers building the robot—tight feedback loops, reproducible runs, and honest reports. It is not shipping yet. Deep model training is not the starting point; engineering discipline is.

How it works

The platform will connect those foundations into a repeatable evidence loop. Physical test facilities stay on the roadmap.

  1. 1

    Define stories

    Align on user stories that map to real deployments—retail, inspection, logistics, or your vertical.

  2. 2

    Run & grade in simulation

    Execute software versions in simulation worlds; each story receives a grade and diagnostic context.

  3. 3

    Track & improve

    Compare runs over time, prioritize fixes, and show partners measurable progress toward real-world readiness.

  4. 4

    Graduate to physical chaos (when ready)

    Physical test facilities and public-facing venues are on the roadmap—a place for robots to experience real human chaos. That infrastructure does not exist yet; virtual proof comes first.

Who we are

Living Proof Labs is led by Aaron Roller, founder and the human anchor of an AI-native company. Aaron works alongside specialized agents for operations, engineering, and comms—one human, many agents, all focused on shipping verified robotics outcomes.

In the early 2000s, GPS was emerging, and most people had no use for it. Aaron helped adapt it to athletes in the world they already lived in. MotionBased, acquired by Garmin, became the foundation of Garmin Connect—devices still in the wild, serving millions of athletes decades later. Outdoor fitness without GPS is hard to remember. The goal with robots is the same: technology trustworthy enough to be accepted alongside people.
