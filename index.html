Living Proof Labs helps robotics teams prepare for human environments with evidence—not hope. Living Proof Verification™, our open-source product, runs a robot's software through repeatable missions in simulation and publishes a report a person can read: how many runs passed, against what bar, and why the others failed.

Living Proof Verification™

Available today

Living Proof Verification™ is open source and made to help anyone who finds it useful. It runs Unitree robots through simulated missions in MuJoCo and publishes graded reports with the evidence behind every run: the passes, the failures and the bar they are measured against.

How it works

Living Proof Verification™ connects those foundations into a repeatable evidence loop.

  1. 1
    Available now

    Define stories

    Align on user stories that map to real deployments—retail, inspection, logistics, or your vertical.

  2. 2
    Available now

    Run & grade in simulation

    Run a robot's software through the same mission many times in simulation; each run is graded against an agreed bar, with the evidence kept.

  3. 3
    Next

    Track & improve

    Compare runs over time, prioritize fixes, and show partners measurable progress. Living Proof Diagnosis™ brings agents and humans together to review the evidence and code and find a path to a fix.

  4. 4
    Next

    Graduate to the Labs

    A fleet of physical robots running the same missions in the Labs, real places with real human chaos, alongside partner companies who share the vision.

Who we are

Aaron Roller: Human leading Agents improving Robots. Aaron founded Living Proof Labs and works alongside specialized agents for operations, engineering, and design, improving robots by proving their capabilities. Previously Head of Software Infrastructure & Reliability at ANYbotics, still working with the team. Earlier: Airtonomy, AutoModality, Cruise.

In the early 2000s, GPS was emerging, and most people had no use for it. Aaron helped adapt it to athletes in the world they already lived in. MotionBased, acquired by Garmin, became the foundation of Garmin Connect—devices still in the wild, serving millions of athletes decades later. Outdoor fitness without GPS is hard to remember. The goal with robots is the same: technology trustworthy enough to be accepted alongside people.
